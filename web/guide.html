1. What this app does

You bring a folder of art. The app turns it into a finished NFT collection on Stacks (a Bitcoin layer):

  1. Numbers your pieces 0001, 0002 …
  2. Gives each piece traits, a rarity tier and a story, written by an AI running on your own computer from what's actually in the picture. Or you set traits yourself.
  3. Lets you review and edit every piece exactly as wallets will show it.
  4. Stores the art and stories on IPFS (your own node, Pinata, or both).
  5. Writes the contract, which you deploy and mint from your own wallet.

It runs only on your computer at http://127.0.0.1:8130. There's no account, no company server, and no fee to us. You pay only the normal network fees.

3. IPFS: where your art actually lives

This is the part most people get wrong, so it's worth two minutes.

The blockchain doesn't hold your picture. An NFT is a small record that says "token #12 belongs to this person, and its details are at this address." The picture and its story live at that address, on IPFS.

IPFS addresses files by their fingerprint (called a CID, like bafybei…). The same file always has the same CID, and any copy anywhere can answer for it. That's great, with one catch: somebody has to keep a copy online. That's called pinning. If nobody pins your files, your NFTs still exist, but every wallet shows blank boxes.

This really happens. The author of this app lost the art behind several collections when the computer running his IPFS node was wiped. The NFTs were fine; the pictures were gone until he restored them from his own backups. Hosting companies shut down too. Whoever holds the files decides whether your art is visible.

You have three choices:

HowGoodWatch out
🏠 Your own nodeA free program on a computer you own (section 4).Nobody can switch it off. No limits, no fees.The computer should stay on. Back it up.
☁️ PinataA company pins it for you (section 5).Easiest; nothing to run.Free plan is small. Your art depends on your account staying paid and open.
🛡️ BothStored on your node and on Pinata.If either one fails, the other still serves it.A little more setup.

4. Run your own IPFS node

Any computer that stays on works: a mini PC, an old laptop, a home server. The simplest way is Docker (install Docker), then one command:

docker run -d --name ipfs --restart unless-stopped \
  -v ipfs_data:/data/ipfs \
  -p 4001:4001 -p 4001:4001/udp \
  -p 127.0.0.1:5001:5001 \
  -p 127.0.0.1:8080:8080 \
  ipfs/kubo:latest

That's it. In the app's setup, use:

  • IPFS node address (API): http://127.0.0.1:5001
  • Gateway: http://127.0.0.1:8080

Node on a different computer?

Run the minter on the same machine as the node (simplest), or let the node's API listen on your home network by changing 127.0.0.1:5001:5001 to 5001:5001 and, on the node, running docker exec ipfs ipfs config Addresses.API /ip4/0.0.0.0/tcp/5001 then docker restart ipfs. Then use http://<node-ip>:5001 in the app.

Never open port 5001 to the internet. Anyone who can reach it can control your node. Keep it on your own computer or home network. (Port 4001 is the safe one; that's how other IPFS nodes find your files.)

Without Docker

Install Kubo from docs.ipfs.tech, then ipfs init and ipfs daemon (keep it running, or make it a service). IPFS Desktop also works and uses the same addresses.

Can the world see my node?

Wallets fetch your art from public IPFS gateways, which find your node through port 4001. Most home routers allow it automatically; if pictures don't load for others, forward TCP/UDP 4001 to the node. Or skip all of that and give your node a public web address (section 6), or add Pinata as the always-reachable copy.

5. The easy alternative: Pinata

  1. Sign up at pinata.cloud. There's a free plan (check their site for the current limits; bigger collections need a paid plan).
  2. Open API Keys → New Key. Give it the pinFileToIPFS permission (or Admin, simpler), name it, create it.
  3. Copy the long JWT (it starts with eyJ). Pinata shows it only once.
  4. Paste it in the setup wizard and press Test the key.

The key is saved only on your computer, in a file only your user can read, and it's never shown in the browser again.

6. ipfs:// links or your own gateway?

Each piece's metadata points at its picture. There are two ways to write that link:

  • ipfs://bafy…/images/0001.png (recommended). This is the standard: every wallet turns it into a working link with its own gateway, and it never depends on a website of yours. Choose this with Pinata, or when your node is reachable (section 4).
  • https://ipfs.yourdomain.com/ipfs/bafy…/…: through your own public gateway. Useful when your node sits at home behind a router that others can't reach. You'd put your node's gateway (port 8080) behind a web address, for example with a Cloudflare Tunnel (free, no ports to open) or a reverse proxy. The link then depends on that address staying up, but you can repoint it later (section 11).

7. AI stories and rarity (optional)

Install Ollama (free, runs on your own machine), then pull two models:

ollama pull llava          # looks at the pictures
ollama pull qwen2.5:14b    # writes the stories (smaller computer? try llama3.2)

For each piece the AI: describes what's in the picture → picks 5 traits → the app counts how rare each trait is across the whole collection → assigns one of 12 tiers (Mythic, Celestial, Divine, Legendary, Ascendant, Relic, Epic, Rare, Elite, Uncommon, Common, Base) → writes a story that fits the picture and its rarity.

It takes roughly 20–60 seconds per piece with a decent GPU. It remembers its progress, so you can stop and continue. AI makes mistakes: it may call a book a scroll or get a colour wrong. Always review in step 3.

No GPU? Use the manual option: one description for all pieces, plus trait categories with weights (e.g. Background: Gold:5, Night:30, Dawn:65). Rarer values give higher tiers.

9. Making a collection, step by step

  1. Collection and art: type a name, choose all your images, press Add images. They're numbered by file name, so name them in the order you want (01.png, 02.png…). Untick Replace to add more later.
  2. Rarity and stories: optionally give a theme ("guardians of a fallen sky-city") and canon (facts that must stay true), then press Write rarity + stories with AI. Or use the manual option.
  3. Review and edit: press Show my collection, then 👁 Preview and edit on any piece. You see it as a wallet will: name, rarity, story, traits, plus the exact metadata. Fix anything and save.
  4. Store on IPFS: set the royalty and press Store on IPFS + finish the contract. This uploads the art and metadata and puts the final address into your contract. Use preview piece 1 to check.
  5. Deploy and mint: opens the wallet page (next section).

Your collections are saved in ~/SonOfSatoshi-Minter/projects/<Name>/: the images, the metadata, the contract and a state.json with what you've deployed.

10. Deploy and mint

  1. Connect wallet. Make sure the wallet is on the same network as the app (testnet/mainnet). The account you connect becomes the collection's owner: only it can mint or change the collection later. Use an account you'll keep (a Ledger account is ideal).
  2. Deploy. Approve it in the wallet. The page waits until the network confirms, usually 1–10 minutes. Don't mint before that: the contract doesn't exist yet.
  3. Mint. Mints up to 200 per approval, sent to your address. A collection of 1,000 takes 5 approvals; the page waits for each round to confirm, then offers the next. You can close it and come back; it reads the progress from the chain.

Minted NFTs appear in Xverse/Leather after the network and Hiro (the indexer wallets use) catch up, usually minutes.

11. After minting: edit, refresh, royalty, freeze

  • Fix a story or picture: edit it (step 3), press Store on IPFS again (new CID), then on the mint page press Re-point and afterwards Refresh wallets.
  • Refresh wallets: sends the standard "please re-read" notice (SIP-019). Use it when pictures show as blank boxes, or after a re-point. Wallets can take up to an hour to update.
  • Moved your storage (new node, different gateway)? Store again, then Re-point. That's why this contract can be re-pointed: your NFTs never get stranded on a dead address.
  • Royalty: change the % or who receives it.
  • Freeze: locks the art and stories forever, for everyone including you. It proves to collectors that nothing will change. Only do it once everything is final and your storage is solid.
Keep the owner wallet safe. Only the wallet that deployed the contract can re-point, refresh or change it. Lose that wallet and your collection is stuck the way it is, forever.

12. Keep a backup

Copy ~/SonOfSatoshi-Minter/projects/ somewhere safe (an external drive, a NAS). With those files you can put your art back on any IPFS node at the exact same CIDs, because IPFS addresses files by content. Your NFTs work again without touching the blockchain. The folder is the master copy of your work.

13. Costs

This app is free. You pay the Stacks network's fees, set by your wallet and usually small (a fraction of an STX to a few STX): one fee to deploy, one per mint round of up to 200, one per re-point / refresh / royalty change.

Practice for free on testnet: switch your wallet to Testnet, copy your ST… address, and request test STX at the Hiro faucet.

14. Safety

  • This app never asks for your secret words (seed phrase) or private key. If anything ever does, stop.
  • Every transaction opens in your wallet: read it before approving. The mint page shows the full contract code.
  • The app only answers on 127.0.0.1 (your own computer) and refuses requests from other websites.
  • Your Pinata key is stored only in ~/SonOfSatoshi-Minter/config.json, readable only by you.
  • Don't expose your IPFS node's port 5001 to the internet (section 4).

15. When something goes wrong

"can't reach an IPFS node"Is the node running? docker ps should list ipfs. Check the address in Settings.
Blank / gray boxes in the walletGive it an hour after minting. Then check preview piece 1 loads. If it does, press Refresh wallets. If it doesn't, your files aren't reachable: see sections 4 and 6.
"Error fetching contract ABI" / mint fails right after deployThe deploy hasn't confirmed yet. Wait; the page enables Mint when it's live.
Wallet shows the wrong networkSwitch the wallet to match the app's network badge (top right).
"That's a testnet address…"ST… addresses are testnet, SP… are mainnet. Match them to the network you chose.
AI stops with an errorPress the button again: it resumes where it stopped. Check ollama ps; a smaller model helps on small GPUs.
Pinata "HTTP 401/403"The key is wrong or lacks the pinning permission. Make a new one.
Pinata "HTTP 413" or quota errorsYour plan's limit; upgrade, or use your own node.

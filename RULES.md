# Rules for using SonOfSatoshi Minter

This is a tool for artists to own their work from end to end. Use it the way it was meant to be used.

## 1. Only mint what you have the right to mint
Mint your own art, or art you have written permission to mint. Don't mint other people's work, stolen art,
AI copies of a living artist's style sold as theirs, or anything you don't have the rights to. You're responsible for
what you publish on a public blockchain, and it stays there forever.

## 2. No scams
Don't use it for rug-pulls, fake collections, impersonation, pump schemes, or misleading promises to buyers. Be honest
about what a collection is and what holders get. If you plan to **freeze** metadata, say so; if you won't, say that too.

## 3. Be honest about the AI
The AI writes stories and picks traits from what it sees. It can be wrong. Review every piece before you mint, and don't
present AI-written text as something it isn't.

## 4. Practice on testnet first
Run your first collection on **testnet** (free test coins). Mistakes on mainnet cost real STX and can't be undone.

## 5. Your keys are your responsibility
The app never asks for your seed phrase or private key, and you should never type them anywhere. The wallet that
deploys a collection is its **owner**: lose it and the collection can never be changed again. A hardware wallet is best.

## 6. Keep your art online
An NFT points at files on IPFS. If nobody keeps those files online, your collectors see blank boxes. Pin your
collection (your own node, Pinata, or both) and back up your `projects` folder. See the in-app Guide.

## 7. Follow the law where you live
Selling NFTs can involve taxes, consumer rules and securities rules that differ by country. That's on you.

## 8. No warranty, not financial advice
This software is provided **as is**, without any warranty (see [LICENSE](LICENSE), sections 15–16). Nothing in it or in
this repository is financial, legal or tax advice. NFTs can lose all their value.
